The egress credential proxy and run-wide subtree budgets are designs, not
installed features. Existing Lease/network policy and conserved quota
primitives do not prove that credential injection or dollar, VM-second, and
token ceilings are available end to end.

## Egress credential custody

The planned proxy holds upstream credentials outside sandboxes and model
context. An admitted Lease receives a scoped, short-lived capability to call
a named connector. The trusted service checks policy, current execution
fences, and quota before injecting a credential into a bounded upstream call.

Provider network enforcement must make the proxy path unavoidable; a proxy
environment variable is insufficient. Direct sockets, alternate DNS,
redirects, metadata endpoints, and peer traffic must not bypass policy. The
service must also prevent credentials from being reflected into responses,
logs, or artifacts. Unsupported enforcement fails admission instead of
silently weakening isolation.

Lost responses are not proof that an external effect was absent. An uncertain
non-idempotent request keeps its reservation and requires reconciliation;
retry does not automatically resend it. Restoring workspace bytes never
undoes a remote write. Missing externality observations must not be reported
as an all-false, side-effect-free segment.

The package design is `packages/little-compute/docs/egress-credential-proxy.md`.
It specifies policy/capability bindings, dispatch recovery, credential
rotation, and adversarial acceptance tests for P2.

## Subtree budgets

The planned budget follows a root run and all bound descendants, including
retries and failed branches. A fork or restored milestone does not reset its
allowance. Admission reserves rights before work starts; consumption and
release follow exact, replayable effects in the conserved quota ledger.

Budget dimensions remain distinct: currency micros use an explicit rate
card, VM-seconds measure allocated environment time, and tokens bind to
admitted model requests. CPU allocation is not elapsed compute time. Missing
usage stays unknown and uncertain effects remain reserved.

When a ceiling is reached, policy can refuse new work, park the run, or
preempt a branch through ordinary fenced cancellation. Cancellation does not
refund spent cost or release uncertain resources early. Workers must stop
at locally enforceable limits even when disconnected from the control plane.

Cost-to-reach and total run spend are different views. A merge counts shared
ancestral charges once; failed siblings remain in run spend. Reusing a known
milestone prefix is measurable, but the exact price of a hypothetical
restart is not. Neither absent telemetry nor retry selection justifies a
zero-cost or exact-savings claim.

The package design is `packages/little-compute/docs/subtree-budgets.md`.
It covers conservation, enforcement policy, recovery, measurement coverage,
and concurrent PostgreSQL/provider fault tests required before shipping.

## Deployment boundary

Both designs keep one control plane and PostgreSQL as canonical authority.
Future worker hosts contribute capacity through outbound connections; they
do not become schedulers or failover control planes. The designs do not add
parked Vault Cleanup, signed-evidence GC, or release-attestation owners to
the lean tier. New budget persistence belongs in its own versioned stream;
the existing `cell_core` v137 history remains frozen.
