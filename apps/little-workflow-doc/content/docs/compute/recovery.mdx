---
title: Recovery and safe time
description: Why Little Compute expires authority late rather than early during clock or primary uncertainty.
---

Little Compute does not use a sandbox timer or a raw PostgreSQL `now()` call as
proof that durable authority has expired. Claims, sandbox Leases,
reservations, Scope deadlines, Approvals, rate windows, and deletion grace all
use a persisted **safe expiry cut**.

A low-frequency, epoch-fenced sampler holds one PostgreSQL session leadership
lock and compares PostgreSQL time with elapsed monotonic host time. Standby
processes do not steal epochs from a healthy leader. Losing the pinned
connection releases leadership; the successor advances the durable epoch
before it can publish a cut. A sampler restart, failover, forward/backward clock step,
uncertain observation, or long sampling gap pauses destructive expiry and
reusable-capacity reclamation. New work with a deadline is rejected while the
cut is uncertain. An existing renewal is never shortened: it keeps the later
of its current expiry and `safeExpiryCut + requestedTTL`.

The sampler connection must be session-affine. Connect directly to PostgreSQL
or use a proxy in session-pooling mode. PgBouncer transaction and statement
pooling are unsupported because adjacent lock, topology, and sample queries may
run on different backends. Little Compute checks the backend PID and exact
advisory lock before every sample and destroys leadership on any mismatch.

The sampler process and its host monotonic clock form a small trusted safety
leaf. PostgreSQL-owned functions independently observe topology and database
time and cap the accepted proposal, but they cannot prove that a compromised
sampler host reported honest monotonic elapsed. Isolate and harden this process;
never colocate untrusted agent code with it or give sampler credentials to a
sandbox.

Recovery requires stable observations plus a grace period. The cut advances no
faster than trusted monotonic elapsed and never goes backward. A large forward
step remains quarantined until it is safe for every authority issued before
the step; availability may be late, but acknowledged authority cannot expire
early.

The exec gateway uses the same cut for its short owner heartbeat. A takeover
serializes on the exact tenant and Sandbox Exec, increments the owner
generation, and preserves the original canonical Exec/Lease/provider/runtime/
workspace identity even if the Attempt later advances to another workspace
head. Historical transition replay is authority-bearing: it is accepted only
while the receipt still belongs to the current unexpired owner token,
generation, and fence. An expired or replaced owner cannot replay an old send,
ACK, cancellation, or release response.

Recovery also preserves the output durability boundary. Pending page metadata
and ACK intent survive gateway-process loss, while supervisor output remains in
its bounded spool until the matching ACK result commits. A terminal resource
cannot be released merely because process state is terminal; its latest
terminal output page must be durably sink-bound and acknowledged, or the
command must carry the separate proven-not-sent receipt.

## Unknown cost and side effects during recovery

Cost and externality observations are optional because a terminal control-plane
row does not, by itself, prove provider billing or sandbox side effects. Recovery
must preserve that uncertainty: it does not turn missing observations into zero
cost or clean (`false`) flags, and it does not estimate compute cost from wall-clock
elapsed time.

Re-execution is eligible only when the saved milestone contains a complete
externality observation and `networkWrites`, `databaseCommits`, and
`externalApiCalls` are all false. If the observation is missing, recovery keeps
re-execution ineligible; it may still restore workspace bytes and replay a
durable placement receipt, but it cannot claim safe re-execution without an
explicitly trusted capture. A measured zero remains a real observation and is
distinct from an omitted value.

Retry receipts use `reusedPrefixCost` for the measured historical cost prefix
when the selected ancestor has one; this is not an exact counterfactual saving.
They omit that field when cost is unknown, including base-generation fallback.
Legacy receipts that contain the v1 `savedCost` field remain parse-only and
replay-compatible without being rewritten; newer receipts never emit that field.

## Lease-attachment worker recovery

The v46 database authority defines separate worker heads for provider
observation and Lease verification. The CLI and self-host appliance use the
v46 activation/recovery adapter while retaining the frozen v45 cryptographic
verification ABI behind that session-guarded process boundary.

Each process activation owns a dedicated PostgreSQL session guardian and a
generation/fence pair. A successor may adopt still-live claims only after the
database proves the exact prior process incarnation and guardian state; a
contended deterministic lock without that durable proof is hostile contention,
not evidence that the old worker is healthy.

Recovery is paginated and replay-safe. Prior-generation claims settle against
their original ownership and closure lineage and are never projected into the
successor's current material. Insufficient provider evidence returns the item
for re-observation. The migration itself requires both worker role graphs to be
NOLOGIN and drained, plus exclusive role-management maintenance from proof
through commit. Any ambiguous guardian unlock or migration-lock release
destroys the physical pooled connection before it can be reused.

## Lease hibernation and resume

Idle and deadline decisions require a certain safe cut, but provider cleanup
does not stop after a Lease has entered `release_pending`. The reconciler keeps
retrying the exact generation-scoped destroy operation while time is
uncertain. Signed absence or fence evidence may be observed, but reusable
Reservation capacity remains charged until PostgreSQL can validate and commit
that proof against a certain cut.

A completed hibernation seals the accepted Sandbox Exec cut, commits signed
workspace quiescence for the current File Snapshot head, proves the old Lease
and Reservation terminal, returns quota exactly once, and parks the Attempt.
Resume never revives that Lease. It requires the next Attempt-local
Reservation and Lease generations, a current provider allocation operation,
a live signed writable attachment to the exact parked workspace head, and no
competing live right. One owner-held database routine commits the immutable
resume receipt and `parked -> runnable` Attempt compare-and-set atomically.
Wrong workspace heads, forged request digests, uncertain safe time, stale
heartbeats, and duplicate authorities all leave the Attempt parked. Concurrent
replays return the one historical receipt.

The maximum-deadline horizon must cover every admitted Lease, token, Approval,
idempotency tombstone, rate window, retention deadline, and GC grace. The
owner-sealed PostgreSQL policy defaults to 3,650 days
(`315360000000` milliseconds), preserving the public ten-year retention and
idempotency maximum. A sampler loads that persisted version and digest. Policy
change requires a stopped sampler, immutable change evidence, and a successor
epoch; no process-local setting can silently weaken it.

An Operation key's horizon starts again after terminalization. The terminal
transition captures the current frozen safe-cut as an immutable floor; it does
not matter whether the sampler is certain at that moment. A later retry or
reconciler attaches the terminal receipt only after a certain cut has advanced
beyond that floor. Until then the key is undeletable. Garbage collection uses
`max(creationHorizon, terminalSafeCut + declaredTTL)`, never the raw Operation
`terminal_at` timestamp.

The initial cut may start at database time only when Little Compute itself
proves the installation contains no deadline-bearing or idempotency authority.
A populated pre-v5 database with either an existing guard or no guard is
quarantined at the Unix epoch. Immutable evidence records the prior guard and
full deadline inventory. Only an uninterrupted full-policy-horizon monotonic
run can create the receipt that clears quarantine; restart, takeover, clock
anomaly, or primary change restarts that proof from zero.

The M1 exit profile must qualify real two-node primary failover and forward and
backward clock steps at every irreversible time path, while publishing
uncertainty, cut-lag, paused-admission, and late-expiry telemetry. Later M4/M8
profiles requalify the installation's specific HA, STONITH, and provider
bindings; they do not substitute for the baseline M1 proof.

## Backup/restore promotion

`little-compute-ha-backup-restore` packages the physical PostgreSQL
backup/restore gate. Its `qualify-local` operation runs an already-present
PostgreSQL 17 or 18 image through streamed `pg_basebackup`,
`pg_verifybackup`, restore, safe-time uncertainty, stale-authority rejection,
fresh-authority issuance, RPO/RTO measurement, and bounded owned-resource
cleanup. That evidence is intentionally `local_physical` and can never make a
production claim.

`qualify-production` runs the same proof across two distinct named Podman
connections. It writes a run-specific repository only from the source host,
verifies that backup before stopping the source, mounts the repository
read-only on the restore host, and copies it into a restore-owned volume.
Source, restore, and repository identity digests must be pairwise distinct;
the later external attestation still proves their actual failure-domain
separation. The repository is retained rather than deleted so an independent
immutable-store authority can seal and attest it.

Production evidence remains blocked until `verify-promotion` re-reads its
read-only raw artifacts and verifies a separately signed Ed25519 attestation
under an independently pinned trust policy. The attestation binds the exact
evidence, image, portable-HA manifest, source host, restore host, backup
repository, immutable evidence store, policy sequence, revocation epoch, and
validity window. Source, restore, and backup repository must be different
identities and different in every failure-domain class required by policy.
RPO must be exactly zero, measured restore duration must meet both RTO limits,
and evidence retention must remain long enough at verification time.

The raw evidence keeps its external-attestation blocker. Only the derived
create-once promotion receipt is eligible, and release automation must
reproduce it from the evidence, attestation, policy, and current anti-rollback
floors rather than trusting a receipt file by itself.
